The Browser Company will shut down Arc on December 22, 2026. The company emailed Arc members on Thursday, October 22, 2026, with a message signed by Josh, and asked them to move to Dia, its newer browser. After the deadline, Arc for macOS and Windows stops working, Arc accounts are deactivated, and personal data linked to those accounts is deleted within 30 days.

That gives you two months to decide what happens to your tabs, Spaces, passwords and extensions. The details below come from the company's published sunset page and help center, plus independent coverage from TechCrunch, Engadget and 9to5Mac. ## What did The Browser Company announce?

The company will sunset Arc on December 22, 2026. In the message to members, it said the decision was hard to write about after years of building Arc with its community. It also said the team behind Arc is the same team behind Dia.

The message points to security as the reason. Keeping a browser secure takes constant work, and the company says that work is increasing in the age of AI. Dia was built from the ground up for today's security standards, so that is where the engineering and security teams will focus.

The company also made a few claims you can check for yourself: Dia now beats Arc on stability, speed and security, and the browser features Arc members use are free in Dia. Those are company figures, not independent test results.

## Why is Arc being sunset?

Browsers hold passwords, login sessions, extensions and sensitive data. Keeping that software safe is an ongoing job. The Browser Company says it can no longer split its attention between two browsers and keep both at the standard it wants.

So Arc gets a fixed end date, and Dia gets the security team. The company has not published a technical breakdown of what changed under Dia's hood. If security is the reason you are moving, check Dia's release notes and independent reviews rather than relying on the announcement alone.

## Arc sunset timeline: the dates that matter

Here is the schedule from The Browser Company's help center.

| Date | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| October 22, 2026 | The sunset is announced. New Arc accounts can no longer be created. |
| November 23, 2026 | AI features in Arc Search turn off. You can no longer sign in or sync tabs in Arc Search. Arc on macOS and Windows starts showing sunset reminders. |
| December 15, 2026 | Last day to request a copy of your Arc data or ask the company to delete it. |
| December 22, 2026 | Arc for macOS and Windows stops working. Accounts are deactivated, Arc services turn off, and Arc Search for Android is removed from the Play Store and from installed devices. |
| Within 30 days after December 22 | Personal data linked to deactivated Arc accounts is deleted. |

**December 15 is the data request deadline, not December 22.** The company says it will not be able to retrieve Arc user data for data requests after the browser shuts down. If you want a copy of your account data, ask before the 15th.

## What happens to your Arc data?

All Arc accounts are deactivated on December 22, 2026. Once an account is deactivated, the company deletes personal data linked to it within 30 days. If you have both an Arc account and a Dia account, only the Arc account data is affected.

After December 22, you also cannot open Arc to reach data stored on your own device. The company's help center says Arc stops working, so the local data is not a backup you can browse later. Import what you need into Dia, or export your tabs, before the deadline.

## How to move from Arc to Dia

The company says the import takes a few clicks.

### Step 1: Download Dia

Dia is available for macOS and Windows at [diabrowser.com](https://diabrowser.com){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"}. The company says it is free to download.

### Step 2: Pick Arc as the import source

When Dia asks which browser to import from, choose Arc. If you already use Dia, you can still import from Arc at any time before December 22, 2026.

### Step 3: Let Dia copy your setup

According to The Browser Company, the import brings over:

- Your Spaces, which show up in Dia as Profiles
- Favorites, Pinned Tabs and Folders
- Bookmarks
- History
- Passwords
- Extensions

The company also says Spaces, Folders and Tab Groups import with their data intact, on both macOS and Windows.

### Step 4: Check your setup before you stop using Arc

Arc still works until December 22, so run both browsers for a while. Confirm that your logins, extensions and daily workflows behave the way you expect. Then make Dia your default browser.

## Is Dia free, and can you skip the AI?

The company says the browser features Arc members use are free in Dia, and that the AI is optional. That matters if you liked Arc as a way to organize tabs and Spaces and do not want AI in every action.

The announcement does not lay out long-term pricing for Dia's AI features. Treat "free browser, optional AI" as the current position, not a promise about future plans.

## What if you don't want to use Dia?

You do not have to move to Dia. Before December 22, 2026, you can export a list of your Arc tabs and take it to another browser. Arc's October update added export tools for exactly this.

You should also back up anything the export does not cover. Check which extensions you rely on, note any saved logins you want to move by hand, and decide how you will organize your workspace in the new browser.

## What to do before December 22, 2026

A short checklist keeps the move calm.

1. Decide whether you will use Dia or another browser.
2. If you choose Dia, install it and import from Arc now, not in December.
3. If you choose another browser, export your Arc tabs and save the file somewhere you will find it.
4. Check your passwords and extensions after the import.
5. File any data request by December 15, 2026.
6. Keep Arc installed as a fallback until your new setup feels stable, then remove it.

## What this means for the browser market

Arc built a following by treating the browser as a workspace, with Spaces, Folders and Pinned Tabs instead of a long row of tabs. The shutdown shows how quickly priorities can move when security costs and AI features rise at the same time.

For Arc users, the practical impact is simple: a firm date, a free import path, and a replacement from the same team. For everyone else, it is a reminder to check where your browser data lives and how you would get it out if the product changed direction.
