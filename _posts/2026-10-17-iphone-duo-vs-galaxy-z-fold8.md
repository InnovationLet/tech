Apple's first foldable is no longer a rumor. The company unveiled the **iPhone Duo** on September 9, 2026, opened pre-orders on October 16, and plans to start deliveries on October 23. For months the device was discussed as the "iPhone Fold" or "iPhone Ultra," but Apple settled on Duo. Its natural rival is Samsung's **Galaxy Z Fold8**, which launched on July 22, 2026 and has been on sale since early August.

So which one should you buy? Short answer: the Galaxy Z Fold8 is lighter, thinner and cheaper, and it is the more mature foldable today. The iPhone Duo has a newer chip, an IP68 rating, a very large battery-life claim and the Apple ecosystem behind it. This guide walks through both phones using official specifications from Apple and Samsung, plus early comparison data from independent outlets. ## What is the iPhone Duo?

The iPhone Duo is Apple's first foldable phone. It uses a book-style design: a 5.4-inch outer display that works like a normal phone, and a 7.6-inch inner display that opens like a small tablet. Apple says the inner display is 50 percent larger than the one on the iPhone 18 Pro Max, and that the outer display gives 90 percent of the screen area of the iPhone 18 Pro.

A few hardware details from Apple's announcement:

- A frame made from grade 5 titanium, with a hinge built from more than 100 components.
- A nano-texture finish on the inner display, designed to cut glare and make the crease less visible.
- IP68 water and dust resistance.
- A dual-battery design with one battery in each half of the phone.
- An eSIM-only design worldwide, with no physical SIM tray.
- Touch ID in the side button instead of Face ID.

Apple says the Duo will support Apple Pencil with USB-C later this year.

## What is the Samsung Galaxy Z Fold8?

Samsung's 2026 foldable lineup changed shape. Instead of one Fold, there are now two: the **Galaxy Z Fold8** and the **Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra**. The Ultra is the direct successor to the Galaxy Z Fold7, with an 8-inch inner display and a 6.5-inch cover screen. The standard Fold8 is a new, wider and shorter design with a 7.6-inch inner display and a 5.5-inch cover screen, with a 4:3 inner screen ratio.

That distinction matters for this comparison. When people search for "Galaxy Fold 8," they may mean either phone. The standard Fold8 is the closer match to the iPhone Duo in size and layout, while the Fold8 Ultra is the closer match in price and ambition.

According to CNBC's launch coverage, Samsung announced the Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra at $2,099 (a $100 increase over the Fold7), the Galaxy Z Fold8 at $1,899 and the Galaxy Z Flip8 at $1,199.

## Design and build: lighter vs tougher

On paper, Samsung wins the portability contest. At 201g, the Galaxy Z Fold8 is 53g lighter than the Duo's 254g, and it is thinner both open and closed. Independent comparisons note that the Duo is the heaviest iPhone Apple has released, which is likely a result of having a battery and hardware on both sides of the hinge.

Apple's answer is materials and durability engineering. The Duo has a grade 5 titanium enclosure, Ceramic Shield on the back, and Ceramic Shield 2 on the outer display, which Apple says has three times better scratch resistance than the previous generation. Apple also says the inner display layers are built to glide past each other like pages of a book to relieve bend stress.

On water and dust protection, Apple's IP68 rating is stronger than the IP48 listed for the Fold8 Ultra. IP48 means the phone is protected against dust only above a certain particle size, while IP68 covers full dust protection.

One caveat: independent durability testing of the iPhone Duo had not been published as of mid-October 2026. Foldable phones have historically had questions around hinge wear and screen creases, so it is worth waiting for teardown and long-term reviews if you are cautious.

## Display and crease

Both phones open to a 7.6-inch inner screen, so the "big screen" experience is similar. The inner shapes differ slightly. The Fold8 uses a 4:3 style layout with squarer corners, while the Duo has rounded display corners. Apple also says both of its displays share the same aspect ratio, so apps scale proportionally whether the phone is open or closed.

Both displays support high refresh rates. Apple lists ProMotion and 3,000 nits of peak outdoor brightness for the Duo. The Fold8 Ultra is also listed at 3,000 nits peak with a 120Hz refresh rate.

On crease visibility, Apple says its nano-texture finish minimizes how visible the crease is. Samsung's Ultra is described as having a nearly crease-free inner screen. Real-world comparisons will matter more than these claims, and the Duo only reaches buyers' hands on October 23.

## Performance: A20 Pro vs Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5

Apple says the A20 Pro in the Duo is built on a 2nm process, has a 6-core CPU and a 7-core GPU, and is paired with a custom vapor chamber for sustained performance. Apple claims up to 35 percent better sustained performance than the iPhone 17 Pro.

Samsung uses last year's Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 for Galaxy, built on 3nm. It is still among the fastest chips on the Android side. As GSMArena noted in its comparison, we will need benchmark testing before declaring a winner, since Apple's performance claims are manufacturer figures.

For most daily tasks like browsing, messaging, video and social apps, both phones will feel fast. The difference will show up in heavy gaming, long video exports and on-device AI tasks.

## Cameras: similar hardware, different strengths

Both phones skip the dedicated telephoto lens on their standard models.

The iPhone Duo has a 48MP Fusion Main camera and a 48MP Fusion Ultra Wide camera. Apple says the Main camera includes an optical-quality 2x telephoto crop, and the phone can record 4K at 120 frames per second in Dolby Vision. A 12MP Center Stage front camera sits on the outer display, and a separate FaceTime camera is hidden under the inner display.

The Galaxy Z Fold8 uses a 50MP wide camera and a 50MP ultra wide camera. If you want a telephoto lens and a high-resolution main sensor from Samsung, the Fold8 Ultra adds a 200MP wide camera and a 10MP telephoto.

Apple also built camera features around the folding design. Duo Preview shows a live preview on the outer display so the person in front of the camera can check the frame. Smart Take uses on-device AI to capture photos automatically when subjects pose. These are Apple's descriptions, and real photo quality will need independent testing.

## Battery life and charging

This is the hardest category to call, because the two companies report battery data differently.

- **Apple:** does not state the Duo's battery capacity. It claims up to 31 hours of video playback on the inner display, up to 44 hours on the outer display, and up to 24 hours of typical use with both displays used equally. Apple says the phone reaches about 50 percent charge in around 20 minutes with a 60W-capable adapter, and 50 percent in around 30 minutes wirelessly with MagSafe or Qi2.
- **Samsung:** lists a 4,800mAh battery for the Fold8, up to 26 hours of video playback and 45W wired charging. GSMArena's own testing of the Fold8 recorded about 14 hours of active use and 65 percent charge in 20 minutes.

Because these numbers come from different test methods, they do not prove a winner. Wait for independent battery tests of the Duo before treating Apple's claim as settled.

## Software and ecosystem

The Duo runs iOS 27 with a layout redesigned for the folding screen. When open, it shows two Home Screen pages. Apple's Split View lets you run two apps side by side on an iPhone for the first time. Apple also points to Siri AI, which is rolling out in beta, and says third-party apps including Netflix, Zoom and Slack are already adapting to the foldable layout. Apple notes that Siri AI is not initially available in the EU.

The Fold8 runs Android 17 with One UI 9. Samsung has been refining foldable software for years, so multitasking, app continuity and large-screen features are more mature. Samsung lists up to seven major Android upgrades for the Fold8 Ultra. Samsung DeX gives you a desktop-style mode when you connect an external display, which the iPhone Duo does not offer.

In short: if you already use a Mac, iPad, Apple Watch and AirPods, the Duo plugs into that world easily. If you want the most flexible foldable software right now, Samsung has the head start.

## Price and availability

The Duo starts at $1,999 for 256GB. Apple's storage options include 512GB, 1TB and 2TB, and Apple lists installment pricing from $83.29 per month for 24 months. Apple's leasing program starts at $57.99 per month for 24 months. Apple says pre-orders began October 16 in more than 70 countries and regions with availability from October 23, and 28 more countries get it from October 30.

Early reports say Duo pre-orders sold out within minutes, with delivery dates moving to late January. If you want one soon, expect to wait or hunt for retail stock.

The Galaxy Z Fold8 starts at $1,899.99 and is already on shelves. Retailers and Samsung have also offered promotional discounts on the Fold8 Ultra since launch, so watch for deals.

## Who should buy the iPhone Duo?

The Duo makes sense if you:

- Already live inside Apple's ecosystem and want a foldable that fits it.
- Care about water and dust protection (IP68) and a titanium build.
- Value Apple's video recording tools and camera features built for a foldable.
- Do not mind waiting for supply and accept that this is a first-generation product.

## Who should buy the Galaxy Z Fold8?

The Galaxy Z Fold8 makes sense if you:

- Want a lighter, thinner foldable that is easier to carry all day.
- Want a mature foldable experience with proven multitasking and DeX.
- Prefer a lower starting price and immediate availability.
- Use Android and Google services.

If you want the biggest screens and a stronger camera setup from Samsung, step up to the Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra, but expect to pay at least $2,099.99.

## Which foldable should you buy?

There is no universal winner. The Galaxy Z Fold8 is the safer and more practical pick today: lighter, thinner, cheaper and available now. The iPhone Duo is the more ambitious debut: a new chip, a tougher IP68 rating, a dual-battery design and tight Apple integration, but at a higher price, with heavier weight and untested real-world durability.

If you are an iPhone user, the Duo is worth considering once independent reviews arrive. If you are on Android, or you simply want a foldable you can buy and carry comfortably this week, the Fold8 is easier to recommend.
